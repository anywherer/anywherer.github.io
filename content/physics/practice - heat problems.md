### 2. Answer the following specific heat capacity questions

The formula for heat energy ($Q$) is:  
$Q = mc\Delta T$  
where $m$ is mass (kg), $c$ is specific heat capacity (J/(kg·°C)), and $\Delta T$ is the temperature change (°C).

a. The energy required to heat 300 g of water from 10°C to 25°C  
- Mass ($m$) = 300 g = 0.3 kg  
- Specific heat capacity of water ($c$) = 4,186 J/(kg·°C)  
- Temperature change ($\Delta T$) = 25°C - 10°C = 15°C  
- $Q = 0.3 \times 4,186 \times 15$  
- $Q = 0.3 \times 4,186 \times 15 = 18,837 \, J$ or 18.837 kJ  
So, the energy required is 18,837 J.

b. The energy transferred when a 2.5 kg block of ice is brought from -30°C to its melting point (0°C)  
- Mass ($m$) = 2.5 kg  
- Specific heat capacity of ice ($c$) ≈ 2,100 J/(kg·°C) (a standard value, though it can vary slightly)  
- Temperature change ($\Delta T$) = 0°C - (-30°C) = 30°C  
- $Q = 2.5 \times 2,100 \times 30$  
- $Q = 2.5 \times 2,100 \times 30 = 157,500 \, J$ or 157.5 kJ  
So, the energy transferred is 157,500 J.

c. The specific heat capacity of 3.0 kg of an unknown metal if 132,000 J changes its temperature by 10 K  
- Mass ($m$) = 3.0 kg  
- Energy ($Q$) = 132,000 J  
- Temperature change ($\Delta T$) = 10 K (1 K = 1°C, so 10 K = 10°C)  
- $Q = mc\Delta T$  
- $132,000 = 3.0 \times c \times 10$  
- $132,000 = 30c$  
- $c = \frac{132,000}{30} = 4,400 \, J/(kg·°C)$  
So, the specific heat capacity is 4,400 J/(kg·°C).

d. The temperature change of 500 g of water if 450 g of iron at 80°C is placed into the water and the iron drops to 60°C  
- Mass of water ($m_w$) = 500 g = 0.5 kg  
- Specific heat capacity of water ($c_w$) = 4,186 J/(kg·°C)  
- Mass of iron ($m_i$) = 450 g = 0.45 kg  
- Specific heat capacity of iron ($c_i$) = 450 J/(kg·°C)  
- Initial temperature of iron ($T_i$) = 80°C  
- Final temperature of iron ($T_f$) = 60°C  
- Temperature change of iron ($\Delta T_i$) = 60°C - 80°C = -20°C  
- Let the final temperature of the water (and system) be $T_f$ (in °C). The initial temperature of water isn’t given, so I’ll assume it’s at room temperature, say 20°C (a common assumption unless otherwise stated).  
- Temperature change of water ($\Delta T_w$) = $T_f - 20$  
- Heat lost by iron = Heat gained by water (assuming no heat loss to surroundings):  
- $Q_i = Q_w$  
- $m_i \cdot c_i \cdot \Delta T_i = m_w \cdot c_w \cdot \Delta T_w$  
- $0.45 \times 450 \times (-20) = 0.5 \times 4,186 \times (T_f - 20)$  
- $0.45 \times 450 \times (-20) = -4,050 \, J$ (heat lost by iron)  
- $-4,050 = 0.5 \times 4,186 \times (T_f - 20)$  
- $-4,050 = 2,093 \times (T_f - 20)$  
- $T_f - 20 = \frac{-4,050}{2,093} \approx -1.935$  
- $T_f = 20 - 1.935 \approx 18.065 \, °C$  
- Temperature change of water ($\Delta T_w$) = $18.065 - 20 = -1.935 \, °C$  
So, the temperature change of the water is approximately -1.94°C (it decreases).

e. 1.0 kg of water reaches thermal equilibrium with 100 g of iron. What is the final temperature if the water was initially at 15°C and the iron was at 1000°C?  
- Mass of water ($m_w$) = 1.0 kg  
- Specific heat capacity of water ($c_w$) = 4,186 J/(kg·°C)  
- Mass of iron ($m_i$) = 100 g = 0.1 kg  
- Specific heat capacity of iron ($c_i$) = 450 J/(kg·°C)  
- Initial temperature of water ($T_{w,initial}$) = 15°C  
- Initial temperature of iron ($T_{i,initial}$) = 1000°C  
- Let the final temperature be $T_f$ (in °C).  
- Heat lost by iron = Heat gained by water:  
- $m_i \cdot c_i \cdot (T_{i,initial} - T_f) = m_w \cdot c_w \cdot (T_f - T_{w,initial})$  
- $0.1 \times 450 \times (1000 - T_f) = 1.0 \times 4,186 \times (T_f - 15)$  
- $45 \times (1000 - T_f) = 4,186 \times (T_f - 15)$  
- $45,000 - 45T_f = 4,186T_f - 62,790$  
- $45,000 + 62,790 = 4,186T_f + 45T_f$  
- $107,790 = 4,231T_f$  
- $T_f = \frac{107,790}{4,231} \approx 25.49 \, °C$  
So, the final temperature is approximately 25.5°C.

### 3. Answer the following latent heat questions

The formula for latent heat energy ($Q$) is:  
$Q = m \cdot L$  
where $m$ is mass (kg) and $L$ is the latent heat (J/kg).

a. 3 kg of liquid water is at 100°C. How much energy is required to convert this water to steam?  
- Mass ($m$) = 3 kg  
- Latent heat of vaporization of water ($L$) = 2,260,000 J/kg  
- $Q = 3 \times 2,260,000$  
- $Q = 6,780,000 \, J$ or 6.78 MJ  
So, the energy required is 6,780,000 J.

b. 200 g of an unknown solid takes 1.2 × 10⁵ J of energy to melt. Determine the specific latent heat of fusion for this solid.  
- Mass ($m$) = 200 g = 0.2 kg  
- Energy ($Q$) = 1.2 × 10⁵ J = 120,000 J  
- Latent heat of fusion ($L$) = ?  
- $Q = m \cdot L$  
- $120,000 = 0.2 \times L$  
- $L = \frac{120,000}{0.2} = 600,000 \, J/kg$  
So, the specific latent heat of fusion is 600,000 J/kg.

c. A pipe runs 1 kg of steam at 100°C through a large bath of water. If 2.0 × 10⁶ J of energy is removed from the steam, does steam come out the other end?  
- Mass ($m$) = 1 kg  
- Latent heat of vaporization of water ($L$) = 2,260,000 J/kg  
- Energy removed ($Q$) = 2.0 × 10⁶ J = 2,000,000 J  
- Energy required to condense 1 kg of steam to water at 100°C = $m \cdot L = 1 \times 2,260,000 = 2,260,000 \, J$  
Since 2,000,000 J < 2,260,000 J, only part of the steam will condense. The remaining energy needed to fully condense the steam is:  
- $2,260,000 - 2,000,000 = 260,000 \, J$  
Since some energy is still needed to fully condense the steam, not all of it will condense, so steam will come out the other end.

### 4. Answer the following specific heat capacity and latent heat questions

a. 300 kg of snow at -10°C on a street needs to be melted. How much energy is needed?  
- Snow is essentially ice, so we’ll use the specific heat capacity of ice and latent heat of fusion.  
- Mass ($m$) = 300 kg  
- Specific heat capacity of ice ($c_{ice}$) ≈ 2,100 J/(kg·°C)  
- Latent heat of fusion of water ($L_f$) = 334,000 J/kg  
- Temperature change to reach 0°C ($\Delta T$) = 0°C - (-10°C) = 10°C  
- Energy to heat snow from -10°C to 0°C:  
- $Q_1 = m \cdot c_{ice} \cdot \Delta T = 300 \times 2,100 \times 10 = 6,300,000 \, J$  
- Energy to melt ice at 0°C to water at 0°C:  
- $Q_2 = m \cdot L_f = 300 \times 334,000 = 100,200,000 \, J$  
- Total energy:  
- $Q_{total} = Q_1 + Q_2 = 6,300,000 + 100,200,000 = 106,500,000 \, J$ or 106.5 MJ  
So, the energy needed is 106,500,000 J.

b. A method used to purify water involves boiling it, collecting the steam, and condensing it back into pure water. Suppose a village needs 100 kg of pure water per day. How much energy is needed to boil the water that begins at 15°C?  
- Mass ($m$) = 100 kg  
- Specific heat capacity of water ($c_w$) = 4,186 J/(kg·°C)  
- Latent heat of vaporization of water ($L_v$) = 2,260,000 J/kg  
- Initial temperature = 15°C, boiling point = 100°C  
- Temperature change ($\Delta T$) = 100°C - 15°C = 85°C  
- Energy to heat water from 15°C to 100°C:  
- $Q_1 = m \cdot c_w \cdot \Delta T = 100 \times 4,186 \times 85 = 35,581,000 \, J$  
- Energy to boil water at 100°C to steam at 100°C:  
- $Q_2 = m \cdot L_v = 100 \times 2,260,000 = 226,000,000 \, J$  
- Total energy:  
- $Q_{total} = Q_1 + Q_2 = 35,581,000 + 226,000,000 = 261,581,000 \, J$ or 261.58 MJ  
So, the energy needed is 261,581,000 J per day.

c. In about 10 billion years, our star will expand into a red giant. At this time, it will boil away Earth’s oceans. There is 1.4 × 10²¹ kg of water on Earth. Assume it is all pure liquid water. How much energy is required to boil Earth’s oceans if the average temperature of Earth’s water is 17°C?  
- Mass ($m$) = 1.4 × 10²¹ kg  
- Specific heat capacity of water ($c_w$) = 4,186 J/(kg·°C)  
- Latent heat of vaporization of water ($L_v$) = 2,260,000 J/kg  
- Initial temperature = 17°C, boiling point = 100°C  
- Temperature change ($\Delta T$) = 100°C - 17°C = 83°C  
- Energy to heat water from 17°C to 100°C:  
- $Q_1 = m \cdot c_w \cdot \Delta T = (1.4 \times 10^{21}) \times 4,186 \times 83$  
- $Q_1 = 1.4 \times 4,186 \times 83 \times 10^{21}$  
- $4,186 \times 83 = 347,438$  
- $1.4 \times 347,438 = 486,413.2$  
- $Q_1 = 486,413.2 \times 10^{21} = 4.864132 \times 10^{23} \, J$  
- Energy to boil water at 100°C to steam at 100°C:  
- $Q_2 = m \cdot L_v = (1.4 \times 10^{21}) \times 2,260,000$  
- $2,260,000 \times 1.4 = 3,164,000$  
- $Q_2 = 3,164,000 \times 10^{21} = 3.164 \times 10^{24} \, J$  
- Total energy:  
- $Q_{total} = Q_1 + Q_2 = 4.864132 \times 10^{23} + 3.164 \times 10^{24}$  
- $3.164 \times 10^{24} = 31.64 \times 10^{23}$  
- $Q_{total} = (4.864132 + 31.64) \times 10^{23}$  
- $4.864132 + 31.64 = 36.504132$  
- $Q_{total} = 36.504132 \times 10^{23} \, J$ or approximately 3.65 × 10²⁴ J  
So, the energy required is approximately 3.65 × 10²⁴ J.

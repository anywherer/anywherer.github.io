Morphology studies parts of words, and how words are formed from smaller units of meaning called morphemes.

Each word consists of at least one morpheme, *the* smallest unit of meaning.


## morpheme
A morpheme is a string of letters that can't have any letters removed and still retain its meaning.

```anki
A morpheme is ==a string of letters that can't have any letters removed and still retain its meaning.==
```


### root morpheme
The root of a word is one morpheme that contains the primary meaning of the word.

- In the word "unbreakable," the root is "break."
- In the word "biology," the root is "bio" (life) and "logy" (study of).
- In the word "transport" the root is "port" (to carry).

### stem morpheme
Each time a morpheme is taken away from a word, the remaining morphemes are a stem morpheme.



- In the word "deconstruct", the root is "struct" (to build) and the stems are "construct" and "struct".



Inflectional morphemes don't actually change the meaning of things, instead have a grammatical function.



## affix

An affix is a word part that is attaches to a root word (also a stem) to modify its meaning or function.
